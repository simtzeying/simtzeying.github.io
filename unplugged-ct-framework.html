Unplugged Computational Thinking Activities Framework

A nationally funded research project that developed and validated a framework for unplugged computational thinking activities — designed to build programming skills and interest among novice learners without requiring a single device.

The problem

When novice programmers struggle, the instinct is often to give them more coding practice. But research — and classroom observation — suggests the real gap is earlier: students who have never been taught to think computationally before being expected to write code are set up to fail.

Computational thinking — the ability to break problems into steps, recognise patterns, and design logical solutions — is the foundational skill that programming builds on. Yet it is rarely taught explicitly, and almost never in schools that lack computer access.

This project asked a simple but important question: can we teach computational thinking effectively, without any devices at all? And if we can, what does that look like in practice?

What we did

Funded by the Fundamental Research Grant Scheme (FRGS) — Malaysia's national competitive research grant for higher education — this project systematically developed and validated a framework for unplugged computational thinking activities targeted at novice programmers.

Working with secondary school and higher education students, the research team designed a series of hands-on, physical activities that teach core CT concepts — sequencing, decomposition, abstraction, pattern recognition — through movement, card sorting, role-play, and group problem-solving. No screens. No devices. No computer laboratory.

The framework was developed iteratively: activities were designed, tested with learners, refined based on feedback and results, and validated through empirical measurement of learning outcomes and student engagement.

Outcomes & evidence

A validated, reusable framework

The primary deliverable: a structured, evidence-based framework for designing and implementing unplugged CT activities — usable by educators without specialist training in computer science.

Improved programming interest and skills

Participating students demonstrated measurable improvements in both computational thinking ability and programming skills, as well as increased interest in pursuing programming further.

Foundation for BEST and Cintana

The framework developed here became the methodological backbone for the BEST Programme and the Cintana–UFG international collaboration — demonstrating its reach and generalisability.

International replication

The approach was subsequently tested across Malaysia and El Salvador through the Cintana collaboration, confirming that the framework holds in different cultural and resource contexts.

What this work made possible

The framework developed in this project did not stay on the shelf. It became the foundation for the BEST Programme — which used its unplugged CT methodology to reach 200+ students in three Malaysian schools — and for the Cintana–UFG international collaboration, which replicated and validated the approach in El Salvador. It is also the methodological basis for the microMaths programme currently in development. Research that starts with a framework can change how students are taught for years to come.

Interested in this framework or collaborating on related research?

The framework is available to educators and researchers. If you are interested in implementing unplugged CT activities in your school or institution, or in collaborating on follow-on research, please get in touch.
