The BEST Programme

Blended Education for STEM Teaching — a two-phase research programme using low-cost, device-optional computing activities to bring quality programming education to lower secondary students in under-resourced schools across Malaysia and beyond.

Programme background

The BEST Programme began with an observation I could not ignore. Early in my university teaching career, I noticed that my first-year programming students' marks did not form the expected bell curve — they formed a U-shape. Students scoring As had been exposed to programming in secondary school. Those with Ds and Fs had not. The difference was not ability. It was access.

Computer Science is an elective subject at SPM level in Malaysia, and the schools that offer it tend to be those already equipped with computer laboratories and specialist teachers. Under-resourced national schools — where the majority of Malaysian children study — often have neither. The result is a structural disadvantage built into the education system before a student ever reaches university.

"The goal of BEST is straightforward: a student should not arrive at university at a disadvantage because of where they grew up or which school they attended. Quality computing education should not be a privilege."

BEST — Blended Education for STEM Teaching — was designed to meet schools where they are. Using the BBC micro:bit, a low-cost programmable device small enough to fit in a pocket, BEST delivers hands-on programming education without requiring a computer laboratory, specialist infrastructure, or prior teacher training in computer science.

Phase 1 · Completed International Science Partnerships Fund (ISPF) & Sunway University  ·  £80,000 + RM 20,000
2025 – 2026

What we did

In partnership with Lancaster University, UK, we designed and delivered a structured programming curriculum across three lower secondary schools in Malaysia. Students worked through a progressive series of activities using BBC micro:bit devices — starting from basic logic and sequencing, building toward simple but functional applications.

The approach was deliberately blended: digital activity on the micro:bit, combined with unplugged (non-device) computational thinking exercises that could be completed anywhere in the classroom. No computer lab. No specialist software. No prior coding experience required from students or teachers.

Outcomes & evidence

Improved programming understanding

More than 200 lower secondary students demonstrated measurable improvement in programming concepts and computational problem-solving across all three participating schools.

Schools independently asked for more

In an outcome that exceeded all expectations, all three schools independently approached the research team to request a mathematics equivalent — completely unprompted, with no suggestion from the programme team.

The seed of microMaths

That unsolicited request became the empirical basis for microMaths — a forthcoming programme applying the same approach to mathematics education in under-resourced schools.

Phase 2 · In progress Malaysia Science Endowment  ·  RM 417,690
2026 – 2028

What we are doing

Phase 2 shifts the programme's focus from students to teachers. The most sustainable model for school-based STEM education is one where teachers can deliver it confidently and independently — without requiring ongoing support from a university research team.

Working with Teach for Malaysia and the Penang Science Cluster, we are engaging schools, building a network of educators aligned with the programme's values, and developing a curriculum module that gives teachers a structured, ready-to-use resource for delivering BEST activities in their own classrooms. Teacher capacity building and curriculum module development are both currently underway.

Industry partnership — Cytron Sdn Bhd

A key challenge in scaling BEST is hardware cost. Working with Cytron Sdn Bhd — a Malaysian electronics manufacturer — we are designing and validating the Arcade Shield locally, based on requirements gathered during Phase 1. Pilot validation with teachers and students is currently underway. By manufacturing in Malaysia rather than importing, we reduce the cost barrier for end-users and develop Malaysian capability in education technology hardware.

Work in progress

Teacher capacity building

Ongoing engagement with teachers through Teach for Malaysia and Penang Science Cluster — equipping educators to deliver BEST activities independently. Currently in progress.

Curriculum module for teachers

A structured, ready-to-use curriculum module is being developed to support teachers in delivering the BEST programme in their own schools, with or without external research team involvement.

Arcade Shield pilot validation

Pilot testing of the locally manufactured Arcade Shield (developed with Cytron Sdn Bhd) with teachers and students is underway, ahead of wider rollout.

A replicable programme model

A documented, evidence-based model — hardware, curriculum, and teacher support — that other regions and countries can adapt and adopt without starting from scratch.

What comes next — microMaths

The most meaningful outcome of BEST Phase 1 was not planned. When all three participating schools independently asked for a mathematics equivalent of the programme, it confirmed something the research team had hoped but not assumed: the approach works, and teachers and students want more of it. That request is now being answered. microMaths — applying BEST's low-resource model to mathematics education — is in development, with BEST Phase 2 providing the teacher network, hardware infrastructure, and evidence base it needs to launch.

Want to bring BEST to your school, region, or country?

We are actively looking for like-minded partners — schools, education organisations, funders, and researchers who believe quality STEM education should not be a privilege. Phase 2 is open to collaboration.
