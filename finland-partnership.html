Team Finland Partnership

A long-running research collaboration between Sunway University, LUT University, and Turku University (Finland), funded by the Team Finland Knowledge Grant — exploring how hackathons and innovation challenges can build sustainability mindsets among engineering and computing students.

Partnership background

Sustainability is one of the defining challenges of this generation — and universities have a responsibility to produce graduates who not only understand it intellectually, but are equipped to act on it. This partnership, formed with LUT University in Finland under the Team Finland Knowledge Grant programme, set out to explore a specific question: can structured, time-limited innovation challenges — hackathons — effectively build a sustainability mindset among students in engineering and computing?

Finland has deep expertise in education innovation and sustainability-oriented pedagogy. Malaysia is a rapidly developing economy navigating the tension between growth and environmental responsibility. The partnership brought these two perspectives together to develop, test, and refine a model for sustainability education that is active, participatory, and grounded in real-world problem-solving.

"Telling students why sustainability matters is not enough. They need to experience what it feels like to design for it — under pressure, with real constraints, and with other people who see the problem differently."

SustHack · Completed Team Finland Knowledge Grant  ·  RM 320,000
2019 – 2023

SustHack — Hackathons as a model for sustainability mindset in ICT education

What we did

SustHack developed and tested a hackathon model specifically designed to build sustainability mindset among engineering and computing students. Unlike conventional hackathons focused on building products quickly, SustHack framed challenges around real sustainability problems — requiring students to consider environmental, social, and economic dimensions of their solutions.

The model was implemented across both Malaysia and Finland, allowing the research team to study how students from different educational cultures engaged with sustainability-oriented innovation. Teams were given constrained timeframes, real problem sponsors, and interdisciplinary groupings — deliberately designed to mirror real-world innovation conditions.

Outcomes & evidence

Hackathons cultivate sustainability mindset

Participating students demonstrated measurable shifts in sustainability mindset following hackathon participation — including greater awareness of social and environmental dimensions of technology design.

Learning by doing works

The "learning by doing" model of the hackathon proved more effective at embedding sustainability thinking than lecture-based or case-study approaches — students engaged more deeply when solving real problems under real constraints.

A replicable hackathon model

The SustHack model was documented and published, providing a structured template that other institutions can adopt to embed sustainability education into engineering and computing curricula.

Impact XChange · Completed Team Finland Knowledge Grant  ·  RM 320,000
2023 – 2025

Impact XChange — Citizen participation in innovating for sustainable urban development

What we did

Impact XChange extended the SustHack model beyond the university campus — bringing in community stakeholders and framing challenges around sustainable urban development. The project explored hackathons not just as a pedagogical tool, but as a model of citizen participation: a structured mechanism for communities, students, and organisations to co-design solutions to shared sustainability challenges.

The collaboration expanded to include Turku University, bringing additional Finnish expertise in urban sustainability and participatory design. Challenges were co-developed with local partners in both Malaysia and Finland, and outcomes were assessed not only in terms of student learning, but also in terms of the viability and social value of the solutions produced.

Outcomes & evidence

Hackathons as civic participation

The project demonstrated that well-designed hackathons can function as genuine mechanisms for citizen participation — producing solutions with real potential for urban sustainability impact, not just student learning artefacts.

Cross-cultural innovation model

The Malaysia–Finland partnership produced a model that bridges different cultural approaches to sustainability and innovation — with implications for international education collaboration more broadly.

Published in Routledge Taylor and Francis

Findings documented in a chapter published by Routledge Taylor and Francis (2025), establishing the SustHack model as a peer-reviewed contribution to sustainability education practice.

The legacy of this partnership

Six years of collaboration between Malaysia and Finland produced more than two funded projects and three publications. It produced a documented, tested model for sustainability education through hackathons — one that other universities, faculties, and education ministries can adopt. The cross-cultural dimension of the partnership is itself part of the contribution: demonstrating that sustainability education benefits from perspectives that span different development contexts, different educational traditions, and different understandings of what "sustainable" means in practice.
