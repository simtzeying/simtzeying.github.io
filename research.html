Research & Projects

Early in my teaching career, I noticed something that would not leave me alone. My first-year programming students' marks formed a U-curve — not the expected bell curve. Students scoring As had been exposed to programming in secondary school. Those with Ds and Fs had not. The difference was not ability. It was access. Computer Science is an elective subject at SPM level, and only a small fraction of Malaysian secondary school students sit it. The schools that offer it tend to be those already equipped with laboratories and trained teachers — under-resourced schools often have neither. That observation became the question I have spent my career trying to answer: how do we make quality computing education available to every student, regardless of where they grow up?
